# Cyryx Labs — Website Rebuild Master Prompt

## Mission

Rebuild the Cyryx Labs corporate website as a premium, cinematic, clear, accessible, and commercially useful website for an AI software and technology company.

Use `Cyryx_Labs_Website_Blueprint.md` as the proposed strategy and content baseline. Read it fully. Read existing repository instructions and official brand assets before implementing.

This is a company website with a product portfolio and technology services. Do not reduce the company to an automation agency, a single pilot offer, a generic consultancy, or a research archive. Do not make products, ten services, research, and company information compete for equal attention on the homepage.

Work on a branch or isolated preview. Do not deploy, change DNS, publish social posts, charge a customer, modify live subscriptions, send outreach, or delete production data without explicit authorization.

## 1. Source precedence and truth

Apply this order:

1. Current founder instructions and explicit approvals.
2. Verified current product and commercial records.
3. Official brand assets and supplied brand documents.
4. The proposed website blueprint.
5. Existing production copy, treated as claims requiring review.
6. External design references, used for principles rather than copying.

The current scope is Cyryx Labs and Onyx, Orbit, and AEXOS. Orbit is in development. Do not invent Orbit's purpose, features, architecture, relationship to other products, price, or launch date.

The public website currently presents Onyx as a free beta and AEXOS as a framework with a free npm core. Verify those access paths before repeating their availability labels. Website text alone is not release evidence.

Do not assume that these products are an integrated suite or that paid subscriptions are available. Preserve product distinctions. Unresolved product details must remain unpublished or explicitly marked as awaiting approval in internal content records.

Legacy content describing retired projects must not continue to define the company. Inventory inbound links and historical content before removing or redirecting any route.

## 2. Initial repository discovery

Inspect the existing framework, dependencies, route configuration, content storage, official asset locations, form handlers, email/CRM integrations, analytics, consent behavior, SEO metadata, domain conventions, test suite, CI, and deployment configuration.

Produce:

- `CURRENT_SITE_AUDIT.md`: factual findings with file/route references, severity, and proposed fix.
- `CONTENT_INVENTORY.csv`: existing route, intent, status, retain/revise/redirect decision, and owner.
- `CLAIMS_REGISTER.csv`: claim, source, evidence, approval owner, verification date, and publication state.
- `PRODUCT_TRUTH_REGISTER.json`: current verified category, audience, availability, access URL, dependencies, price state, limitations, support, and evidence for each product.
- `REDIRECT_PLAN.csv`: preserve existing routes where sensible; no broad redirect to the homepage.

Do not replace the stack merely for aesthetic preference. Document any migration proposal with benefits, risks, SEO implications, and rollback.

## 3. Brand and messaging

Company category: AI software and technology company.

Descriptor: AI products, automation, and technology integration.

One-sentence explanation: Cyryx Labs builds AI software and helps businesses connect AI to their existing systems, automate workflows, and develop custom solutions.

Use two main journeys:

- Explore our products.
- Build AI into your business.

Consulting and managed operations are understandable parts of the services journey. Research supports credibility under Insights and existing research destinations.

Use plain language in primary headings. Technical terminology can appear in the detail appropriate to the audience. Do not use unsupported superlatives, invented outcomes, fabricated reviews, client logos without permission, certifications without evidence, or guaranteed ROI/rankings/revenue.

Ownership language must distinguish customer data, custom deliverables, pre-existing IP, third-party code, and product licenses. Do not promise all product IP to subscribers.

## 4. Target navigation and page contracts

Primary navigation: Products, Solutions, Demos, Insights, Company. Persistent CTA: Discuss your project.

Required content architecture:

- Home with the eight-section sequence in the blueprint.
- Products overview with actual status and distinct product paths.
- Onyx and AEXOS detail pages based on verified product facts.
- Orbit preview only after its one-line purpose is approved; in-development status is mandatory.
- Solutions overview grouped into AI strategy & consulting; automation & integration; custom AI software; managed AI operations.
- Preserve useful specialist service pages and URLs.
- Demos with client/internal/demo/concept labels and limitations.
- Company with truthful founder and organization information.
- Insights and research without orphaning existing articles.
- Trust/security information with implemented controls distinguished from policies or plans.
- Project inquiry, privacy, and applicable terms.

Do not ship empty sections to suggest scale. Do not route every interaction to one consulting form. No login link without a working destination. No price page or checkout for an unapproved offer.

## 5. Homepage copy and order

Use the blueprint's draft copy as the working baseline. Do not replace it with generic futurist filler.

Hero:

Eyebrow: AI PRODUCTS & TECHNOLOGY SERVICES

Headline:
AI products and automation.
Built for the way you work.

Body:
We build AI software, connect your existing systems, and automate the work between them. Explore our products or work with us—from strategy and implementation to ongoing support.

Primary CTA: Discuss your project.
Secondary CTA: Explore our products.
Support line: Clear scope. Visible progress. Defined controls.

Sequence:

1. Hero: meaning and action available immediately.
2. Two starting points: products and custom solutions.
3. Workflow example: understandable input, processing, approval, and result.
4. Four solution groups: concise descriptions and relevant routes.
5. Product portfolio: name, job, audience, status, action.
6. Evidence: clearly labeled product/demo/client work.
7. Delivery and trust: understand, define, build/test, launch/support.
8. Company and final action: accountable identity and useful project invitation.

Avoid repeating a full ten-service inventory, detailed product feature lists, market-statistic blocks, an embedded qualification quiz, and a research catalog on the same homepage. Preserve these only where they serve a specific deeper-page task.

## 6. Art direction: Precision in Motion

Preserve the exact official Cyryx monolith and wordmark. Use real source assets. Do not trace an invented replacement, create a new C-hexagon, alter approved geometry, or substitute a generic text logo for the final site.

Brand reference tokens:

- Onyx: #050607
- Obsidian: #0A0D0F
- Graphite: #11161A
- Gunmetal: #1B2227
- Steel: #8C949E
- Silver: #C7C9CC
- Core Teal: #0F6B68
- Teal Glow: #19C7C0
- Readable near-white may use the supplied HTML token #F5F7F7.

Typography baseline: Space Grotesk for headings, Inter for body/UI, IBM Plex Mono for limited technical labels. The official wordmark retains its own typography. The brand 70/20/8/2 balance is direction, not a strict pixel quota. Contrast and legibility take priority.

Create depth with composition, material, light, and selective motion—not an excess of glowing borders, identical dark cards, particles, or microscopic labels.

The hero may use a carefully art-directed metallic composition, but it must explain the company alongside real content. Never present a generated corporate building or fictional team as the actual company. Actual approved Onyx visuals belong to its product presentation; do not spread generic robot imagery across the brand.

Create desktop and mobile visual targets for the hero, navigation, workflow example, product section, and closing CTA before expanding the component system. Where a visual asset is missing, document the requirement rather than fabricate a final approved asset.

## 7. Motion and fallback behavior

All essential copy and links render immediately in semantic HTML. Never wait for a video, 3D renderer, animation, or font to make the offer understandable.

Use restrained native/CSS motion for ordinary transitions. Inspect the stack before adding animation libraries. Add a timeline library or WebGL only for a specific justified scene, with measured cost and static fallback. Avoid multiple animation systems for the same behavior.

Proposed timing: 140–220 ms for microinteractions, 240–420 ms for section entrances. Keep hero introduction short and nonblocking. Repeated motion requires controls where applicable.

Honor `prefers-reduced-motion`. With reduction enabled, replace nonessential spatial motion with static states or simple nonmoving changes. Do not hide content because an animation did not run.

Use normal scrolling. No forced intro, scroll hijacking, mandatory horizontal scroll journey, autoplay sound, fake loading phase, hover-only information, or essential information baked into canvas/video.

Mobile uses the same information with simpler media. Pause offscreen animation. Consider data-saving preferences where supported. Do not infer battery state through unsupported APIs.

## 8. Workflow demonstration integrity

Create a deterministic illustrative example using sample data. It is not a live integration unless explicitly implemented, authorized, and verified.

Example path:

Inquiry received → request summarized → CRM record prepared → draft reviewed → approved response sent.

Provide accessible step selection, replay, final-state viewing, and text equivalent. Display the sample-data label visibly, not only in a tooltip.

A simulated approval does not authorize a real email or CRM write. No real credentials, personal data, or external operations in the public demonstration. Do not use fake time-saved or revenue counters.

## 9. Commercial and inquiry behavior

Implement a short project inquiry with explicit intent, reply address, and a short project description. Additional fields should be necessary or optional. Do not request confidential uploads at first contact.

Forms require server-side validation, spam/rate controls appropriate to the stack, accessible inline errors, preservation of entered data, and a real confirmation only after successful receipt. Secrets remain server-side. Document actual routing and test it in a safe environment.

Make optional marketing consent separate from project inquiry. Do not add someone to a newsletter automatically. Explain the next step and use response-time promises only when the owner approves an operational commitment.

Subscription purchase paths belong to specific products with approved offers. Billing, entitlement provisioning, cancellation, support, limitations, and applicable terms must work before enabling paid CTAs. Do not silently change payment or invoicing architecture in this redesign.

A website assistant is optional. It must rely on approved content, disclose uncertainty, offer normal navigation, and not collect confidential information or invent binding commercial statements.

## 10. Evidence and content models

Create reusable content structures for products, services, demos, insights, and claims. Suggested fields:

- Product: id, name, category, audience, core_job, status, verified_at, evidence_refs, limitations, requirements, pricing_state, access_url, support_url, CTA label and destination.
- Service: id, problem, fit, deliverables, inputs, exclusions, acceptance_approach, related_demo, CTA.
- Demo: id, type (client/internal product/internal demo/concept), source_asset, sample_data flag, what_is_real, what_is_simulated, constraints, verified_at.
- Claim: id, wording, scope, source, evidence, approver, approved_at, review_due, publication_state.

Treat these as logical models, not a mandate to add a database or CMS. Use the project's existing architecture where suitable.

## 11. SEO and continuity

Inventory existing URLs, canonical host, metadata, page titles, structured data, sitemap, robots directives, redirects, and social previews.

Keep meaningful URL equity. Make redirects explicit and permanent only when appropriate. Do not ship staging `noindex` settings into production or remove privacy/terms/contact links.

Use truthful Organization and applicable product/service structured data. Do not publish fake AggregateRating, offer prices, or availability. Preserve approved existing post-deploy search submission behavior where present; test changes instead of assuming it works.

## 12. Test and acceptance plan

Test navigation and forms at 360, 390, 768, 1024, and 1440 CSS-pixel viewport widths. Include keyboard-only and reduced-motion runs. Test representative mobile browsers/devices when available; distinguish emulation from real-device evidence.

Quality gates:

- WCAG 2.2 AA target with automated and manual checks; no claim of certification from a scanner.
- Normal-text contrast at least 4.5:1 and large-text contrast at least 3:1 under the applicable criterion.
- Prefer 44px primary touch controls; distinguish this design preference from WCAG AA's 24px minimum criterion and its exceptions.
- Target field LCP ≤2.5 s, INP ≤200 ms, CLS ≤0.1 at p75, segmented mobile/desktop.
- Lab performance checks before launch; field compliance remains unverified until enough real data exists.
- No broken route, dead CTA, hidden content after animation failure, horizontal overflow, or inaccessible modal/dropdown.
- Working error, loading, success, retry, and empty states for real forms and dynamic content.
- Verified product status and approved claims.
- Correct source labels for internal demonstrations and concepts.
- End-to-end inquiry routing evidence, not merely a green success toast.
- No real external sends or paid transactions during testing without explicit authorization.

Suggested directional usability tasks: explain what Cyryx does; find a suitable product; determine Orbit's availability; find help for connecting CRM and email; understand a demo; submit a safe test inquiry. Record results, confusion, and repair decisions. Small tests are qualitative evidence, not population-level conversion proof.

## 13. Analytics

Track separate product and service paths. Use privacy-conscious event properties.

Examples: intent_selected, product_viewed, demo_started, demo_completed, project_form_started, project_form_submitted, meeting_booked, product_activation_confirmed, subscription_confirmed.

Actual booking/payment/activation events require authoritative confirmation. Do not treat button clicks as revenue. Avoid sending raw form text, email addresses, or confidential data to analytics by default.

## 14. Delivery package and completion report

Return:

1. Evidence-backed audit and content inventory.
2. Positioning, IA, and approved-copy changes.
3. Visual direction and responsive targets.
4. Implemented preview/code changes with affected paths.
5. Claims and product truth registers.
6. Redirect/migration plan.
7. Test evidence with PASS / FAIL / NOT RUN, environment, and limitations.
8. Deployment and rollback instructions; no unauthorized production release.
9. Remaining approval items with exact blockers, without presenting unverified features as complete.

Do not say the website is finished because it builds. Completion requires real navigation, content integrity, submission behavior, accessible interaction, responsive checks, and documented performance evidence.

The outcome is not simply a more animated website. It is a company that a first-time visitor can understand, evaluate, and engage with confidently.
